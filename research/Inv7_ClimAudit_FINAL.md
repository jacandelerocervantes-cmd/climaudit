# Verification, Registration, and Transparency of Carbon Credits via Blockchain and Artificial Intelligence: State of the Art, Frontier R&D, and Strategic Blueprint for ClimAudit

## Deliverable 1: State of the Art (research/STATE-OF-THE-ART.md)

### Executive Summary

The voluntary carbon market (VCM) is contracting in value while it restructures around integrity. Ecosystem Marketplace reported a 2023 transaction value of about $723 million, down 61% year over year, with transaction volume down 56% (as summarized by Forest Trends). Independent scientific evaluations published between 2021 and 2025 indicate that traditional crediting methodologies rely heavily on ex-ante baselines and unverified default parameters that are open to over-crediting. In avoided deforestation (REDD+), West et al. (2023) found that only about 6% of the ex-ante offsets in their sample (5.5 of 89 million) were likely associated with additional emission reductions, implying that roughly 94% were not; a later pantropical evaluation (Tang et al., 2025) found that 21 of 66 project units had significantly lower deforestation than expected while still documenting persistent over-crediting. For cookstoves, Gill-Wiehl, Kammen and Haya (2024) estimated that their sample was over-credited 9.2 times on average, whereas Gold Standard's metered methodology, which measures fuel use directly, was closest to their estimates at about 1.5 times over-credited. First-generation permissionless tokenization (Toucan Protocol and KlimaDAO, 2021–2022) drew sustained criticism because older, low-integrity Verra credits were bridged into a single fungible token pool; Verra responded in May 2022 by prohibiting tokens based on retired credits, and Gold Standard bars tokenization of its credits without express written consent.

By October 2026, high-integrity activity is concentrated in durable carbon dioxide removal (CDR), including biochar. S&P Global reported U.S. biochar credit prices of about $150 per tonne for 2025 delivery and about $148 per tonne for 2026 delivery (October 2025), and the Nasdaq/Puro CORC index benchmark was about €130 per tonne in 2026 (as reported by Senken). A market-research vendor (Fortune Business Insights) estimates the biochar market at USD 859.04 million in 2025, USD 969.29 million in 2026 and USD 2.73 billion by 2034 (CAGR 13.83%) [VERIFICAR MANUALMENTE]. Verra's biochar methodology VM0044 v1.2 became active on June 27, 2025 (a minor revision of v1.1 that adds an investment analysis step to the additionality demonstration and aligns the methodology with the ICVCM Core Carbon Principles), and the Integrity Council for the Voluntary Carbon Market (ICVCM) approved VM0044 v1.2 and VM0045 v1.2 as CCP-eligible on August 13, 2025. A major revision, VM0044 v2.0, was open for public consultation from July 15 to August 17, 2026; it proposes extending eligibility to existing biochar facilities, adding monitoring approaches for the hydrogen-to-organic-carbon ratio ($\mathrm{H/C_{org}}$) and moisture content, procedures for feedstock transport emissions, requirements for safe storage and transport of biochar, and uncertainty analysis. Verra integrated Hedera Guardian with its Project Hub, announced on May 28, 2025. Hedera Guardian publishes a VM0044 demonstration policy that uses CDM-derived Tools 03, 05 and 12, and Guardian releases 3.7.0 and 3.7.1 appear on the Guardian platform-updates page dated August 17, 2026.

For AI-assisted compliance review, the Climate Finance Bench (Mankour et al., 2025, arXiv:2505.22752) evaluated seven LLMs on 330 expert-validated question-answer pairs drawn from 33 corporate sustainability reports (not carbon-credit methodologies). With hybrid retrieval (BM25 plus reranking), Claude 3.5 Sonnet was correct on 62% of questions and DeepSeek R1 on 60%, with an error rate of roughly 25–30% for the best configurations; the authors identify retrieval quality as the main bottleneck. Document-ingesting agents are exposed to indirect prompt injection (OWASP LLM01:2025). Hackett et al. (2025, arXiv:2504.11168) reported up to 100% evasion success in some instances against six prompt-injection and jailbreak detection systems, including Microsoft Azure Prompt Shield and Meta Prompt Guard, using character-injection and adversarial machine-learning techniques. These results motivate a design that separates semantic extraction from numerical execution: a read-only LLM extracts structured parameters with passage-level citations, a deterministic checker verifies every figure against source evidence, deterministic code executes the methodology equations, and an authorized human verifier signs the resulting Verifiable Credential (VC).

The IEEE Global ClimateChain Hackathon 2026 runs from October 5 to October 25, 2026 (submission deadline October 25, 2026, 11:59 PM UTC), with a Carbon Markets & Emissions Trading track; required deliverables include a working prototype (testnet deployment is acceptable), a source repository with documentation, an architecture diagram, a 3-minute video demo and a security and data integrity assessment.

For the ClimAudit prototype, the analysis supports the following five architectural recommendations.

1. **Methodology and parameters (Acceptance Criterion a).** Use Verra VM0044 v1.2 for workflow structure and boundary accounting, following the structure of the Hedera Guardian VM0044 policy (Methodological Tools 03, 05 and 12). For biochar permanence, use the temperature- and $\mathrm{H/C_{org}}$-dependent permanence estimate of Woolf et al. (2021), which Puro.earth's 2022 edition followed, and consider an explicit conservative uncertainty discount of the kind used in Isometric's protocols [VERIFICAR MANUALMENTE].
2. **Figure verification and inconsistency detection (Acceptance Criterion b).** Implement a deterministic verification gate that cross-checks every LLM-extracted number against source-document substrings, enforces physical invariants (for example, an $\mathrm{H/C_{org}}$ upper bound, with the threshold taken from the applicable methodology [VERIFICAR MANUALMENTE], and a dry-mass moisture deduction), and runs a first-digit Benford's Law test ($\chi^2$ and Mean Absolute Deviation) across multi-batch production logs as a screening signal rather than proof of fraud.
3. **Claims governance (Acceptance Criterion c).** Position ClimAudit as a pre-verification copilot and evidence pipeline that feeds a human validation and verification body (VVB), not as an autonomous verifier or a permissionless cross-registry liquidity bridge, consistent with the registry stances described in Section A.3.
4. **RAG versus LoRA scope (Acceptance Criterion d).** Deploy hybrid retrieval (dense semantic retrieval combined with BM25 lexical retrieval and a cross-encoder reranker) over versioned methodology chunks. On the Climate Finance Bench, moving from minimal retrieval to hybrid BM25-plus-reranking retrieval raised Claude 3.5 Sonnet from 54.8% to 62.1% (+7.3 percentage points; BM25 contributed +4.3 and reranking +3.0); the specific 75/25 dense/BM25 weighting is a design choice that the benchmark does not test. Defer LoRA/QLoRA fine-tuning to post-hackathon stages by exporting verified (evidence, extraction, VVB_correction) triples.
5. **Primary differentiation (Acceptance Criterion e).** Center the prototype on a neuro-symbolic evidence checker with provenance-scored uncertainty, linked to Guardian's formula-linked definitions, and add a parcel-period double-counting guard computed as $\mathrm{SHA\text{-}256}(\mathrm{GeoJSON}_{\mathrm{canonical}} \,\|\, \mathrm{start\_date} \,\|\, \mathrm{end\_date})$.

## Section A: Market Realities and Known Failures

### A.1 Documented Problems of Voluntary Carbon Markets

Empirical literature published between 2021 and 2025 has quantified structural failures across major VCM project categories. Unverified baseline assumptions, adverse selection and static default factors are repeatedly identified as drivers of over-crediting. Across forest conservation, compliance forestry and household energy devices, independent evaluations report baseline inflation and undercapitalized risk buffers, and the literature also contains genuine methodological disagreement about how counterfactuals should be constructed.

**Tropical forest conservation (REDD+).** West et al. (2023), "Action needed to make carbon offsets from tropical forest conservation work for climate change mitigation," *Science* 381: 873–877 (doi:10.1126/science.ade3535), applied synthetic control methods to 27 voluntary REDD+ projects in six countries (Peru, Colombia, Democratic Republic of Congo, Tanzania, Zambia and Cambodia). Multi-site projects were assessed site by site (31 sites; 26 sites after exclusions), and 18 projects were used in the carbon-offset analysis. Most projects did not significantly reduce deforestation relative to their synthetic controls, and for those that did, reductions were smaller than claimed. Of 89 million ex-ante offsets, 63.2 million (71%) came from projects without significant reductions and 25.8 million (29%) from projects with some avoided deforestation but less than expected; only about 5.5 million (6.2%) were likely associated with additional emission reductions, which implies that roughly 94% were not. Several analyses contest this result. Guizar-Coutiño et al. (2022), *Conservation Biology* 36(6): e13970, evaluated 40 voluntary REDD+ projects in nine countries using pixel-level matched counterfactuals and reported deforestation reductions at 34 sites, with small increases at six; the same study reports that deforestation within project areas fell by 47% (95% CI 24–68) relative to matched counterfactual pixels. Mitchard et al. (2023, arXiv:2312.06793, a non-peer-reviewed preprint) argued in "Serious errors impair an assessment of forest carbon projects: A rebuttal of West et al. (2023)" that West et al. used poorly chosen data, weak counterfactuals and erroneous calculations. A subsequent pantropical evaluation, Tang et al. (2025), "Tropical forest carbon offsets deliver partial gains amid persistent over-crediting," *Science* 390(6769): 182 (doi:10.1126/science.adw4094; published October 9, 2025), analyzed 52 voluntary REDD+ projects (66 project units) in 12 tropical countries. It found that 21 of 66 units (32%) had significantly less deforestation than expected, that 11 of 66 units (17%) had more deforestation than matched controls, that about 35% of initiatives (18 of 52) reported baselines well above what the data supported, and that an estimated 13.2% of tradable credits reflected real avoided deforestation.

**Compliance forest offsets and buffer pools.** Badgley et al. (2022), "Systematic over-crediting in California's forest carbon offsets program," *Global Change Biology* 28(4): 1433–1445 (doi:10.1111/gcb.15943), found over-crediting in California's compliance program driven by adverse selection around regional average carbon baselines. Coffield et al. (2022), "Using remote sensing to quantify the additional climate benefits of California forest carbon offset projects," *Global Change Biology* 28(22): 6789–6806 (doi:10.1111/gcb.16380), assessed the additional climate benefits of the same project category using remote sensing. On permanence, Badgley et al. (2022), "California's forest carbon offsets buffer pool is severely undercapitalized," *Frontiers in Forests and Global Change* 5 (doi:10.3389/ffgc.2022.930426), reported that wildfire had already consumed at least 95% of the pool's fire-risk reserve in under a decade. For Verra REDD+ projects, a 2023 Berkeley Carbon Trading Project study of 67 projects (Haya, Alford-Jones et al.) found that projects contributed a mean of 2% to the buffer pool to insure all natural risk categories [VERIFICAR MANUALMENTE: full citation of the 2023 report]. A 2025 study by Anderegg et al. in *Global Change Biology*, "Current forest carbon offset buffer pool contributions do not adequately insure against disturbance-driven carbon losses," used data from 125 tropical forest plots and concluded that buffer contributions are substantially undersized, in some cases by more than a factor of 11 [VERIFICAR MANUALMENTE: DOI and the claims about Table 10 of Verra's Non-Permanence Risk Tool and the 50% and 75% buffer reductions].

**Household energy devices.** Gill-Wiehl, Kammen and Haya (2024), "Pervasive over-crediting from cookstove offset methodologies," *Nature Sustainability* 7: 191–202 (doi:10.1038/s41893-023-01259-6), assessed cookstove offset methodologies and estimated that their project sample was over-credited 9.2 times on average. Gold Standard's metered methodology, which directly monitors fuel use, was closest to their estimates at about 1.5 times over-credited, a reduction of about 84% relative to the 9.2-times average. The authors rank the drivers of misalignment as: (1) the fraction of non-renewable biomass ($f_{\mathrm{NRB}}$), (2) firewood-to-charcoal conversion, (3) stove adoption, (4) stove usage, (5) fuel consumption, (6) stacking (use of multiple stoves), (7) rebound and (8) emission factors. The sample size and credit volume (1,213 project activities, about 78.9 million credits as of May 2023, out of 7,933 VCM projects) and the finding that only 4% of cookstove project activities (43 of 992) distributed stoves or fuels meeting the World Health Organization definition of clean cooking as of November 9, 2022 are reported in the literature on this study [VERIFICAR MANUALMENTE]. Verra's cookstove methodology VM0050 v1.0 (*Energy Efficiency and Fuel-Switch Measures in Cookstoves*) became active on October 9, 2024 after a public consultation from December 15, 2023 to January 15, 2024, and the ICVCM has approved it as meeting the Core Carbon Principles. For CCP labels, $f_{\mathrm{NRB}}$ must come from the latest CDM Tool 33 or from the MoFuSS model, fuel consumption must be determined by a Kitchen Performance Test, a Controlled Cooking Test or defaults with fuel-savings cross-checks, and charcoal must use a direct charcoal emission factor or a 4:1 wood-to-charcoal conversion factor.

**Demand side.** Trencher, Nick, Carlson and Johnson (2024), "Demand for low-quality offsets by major companies undermines climate integrity of the voluntary carbon market," *Nature Communications* 15: 6863 (doi:10.1038/s41467-024-51151-w), analyzed the 20 companies that retired the most offsets from 2020 to 2023. None could show that a substantial share of its retired offsets met well-known quality standards, and many bought cheap credits from projects implemented a decade or more earlier.

### A.2 Post-Mortem of First-Generation Credit Tokenization Attempts

Between late 2021 and mid-2022, first-generation Regenerative Finance (ReFi) protocols, most notably Toucan Protocol (Base Carbon Tonnes, BCT) and KlimaDAO (KLIMA), built on-chain carbon markets by bringing Verra Verified Carbon Units (VCUs) onto the Polygon blockchain. Badgley and Cullenward (CarbonPlan, April 7, 2022), "Zombies on the blockchain," documented the mechanics and consequences. KLIMA reached over $1,000 early on and briefly hit the mid-$3,000s, fell to about $100 by mid-January 2022 and was near $20 when the article was written, while BCT traded in the $3–8 range (initially $5–8, later $3–4). Toucan initially made Verra credits with vintages from 2008 onward eligible for BCTs, and nearly all (99.9%) of the Verra credits bridged to BCTs came from projects whose initial crediting period began before 2016, credits that would be ineligible for CORSIA. Applying the Paris Agreement Article 6 rule that excludes projects registered before January 1, 2013 would exclude at least 18.3 million credits (84.8%). The result was that capital flowed to older, low-priced credits rather than to new mitigation.

The bridging workflow was a one-way retire-then-mint process. A user needed a Verra account, retired VCUs and tagged the retirement's beneficiary field with a cryptographic key tied to a blockchain wallet; after Toucan completed its required steps, it issued BCTs to that wallet. Because retirement signifies that a credit's environmental benefit has been consumed against an emissions claim, minting a transferable token from a retired credit separated the on-chain asset from the registry of record. Lavayssière (2025), "Legal Structures of Tokenised Assets," *European Journal of Risk Regulation* (published online May 15, 2025), provides a general legal taxonomy that distinguishes "incomplete tokenisation" (tokens acting as "digital twins" with limited or no legal value) from "complete tokenisation" (tokens embodying legally enforceable rights, either directly or indirectly); the paper does not itself analyze Toucan. A peer-reviewed synthesis of the episode is cited in the source material as doi:10.3390/jrfm19010028 [VERIFICAR MANUALMENTE].

### A.3 Current Stance of Standards on Tokenization, Registries, and Cross-Registry Bridges

**Verra.** On May 25, 2022, Verra prohibited, with immediate effect, creating tokens or instruments based on retired credits, on the grounds that retirement means the credit's environmental benefit has been consumed, and stated that it would explore "immobilizing" credits in Verra Registry accounts so that they could be tokenized if fraud was prevented and environmental integrity upheld. A public consultation on third-party crypto instruments and tokens ran from August 3 to November 1, 2022, and Verra published the consultation summary on January 17, 2023. Commenters proposed that VCUs backing tokens be immobilised in the registry, that token counts be reconciled against held credits on a 1:1 basis, and that token burning and VCU retirement occur together; some respondents opposed immobilisation altogether. On October 12, 2023, Verra introduced a "corporate emissions inventory accounting" retirement reason option in the Verra Registry. On May 28, 2025, Verra announced that Hedera Guardian had been integrated with its Project Hub; the first project to use the integration, ALLCOT's ABC Mangrove Restoration Project in Senegal, uses a digitized version of VM0033. Verra launched version 5 of the VCS Program on December 16, 2025; most updates affecting project design or implementation take effect on January 1, 2027, while updates to Verra-led processes and Registry procedures are effective immediately.

**Gold Standard.** Since May 2022, Gold Standard's Registry Terms of Use have barred creating tokens or digital assets tied to its credits without express written consent. A 30-day consultation (September 28 to October 28, 2022) on conditions for granting that consent drew input from more than 35 organisations, after which Gold Standard announced a readiness phase involving structured consultations with Web3 companies; the conditions page was last updated on September 11, 2024. Gold Standard also organized an open collaboration funded by Google.org with three working groups (Digital Monitoring, Reporting and Verification, led by ClimateCHECK; Digital Infrastructure and Open APIs, led by the IOTA Foundation; and Digital Assets for Climate Impact, led by Gold Standard), whose final outputs were published on November 29, 2023. Gold Standard launched a pilot programme for digital measurement, reporting and verification (dMRV) on October 10, 2024, running until October 2026. Among dedicated carbon removal standards, Puro.earth and Isometric operate digital-native registries; the ICVCM has confirmed Puro.earth as CCP-eligible and has approved Isometric protocols to use the CCP label.

**ICVCM and regulation.** The ICVCM assesses crediting programs and methodologies against its ten Core Carbon Principles (governance, tracking, transparency, robust independent third-party validation and verification, quantification, additionality, permanence, double counting, sustainable development benefits and safeguards, and net-zero alignment). Its assessment-status page, updated August 2026, lists 13 CCP-eligible programs and 45 CCP-approved methodologies, including VM0044 v1.2, VM0050 v1.0 and Gold Standard's Metered & Measured Energy Cooking Devices methodology. On August 13, 2025, the ICVCM approved Verra's VM0044 v1.2 and VM0045 v1.2 (Improved Forest Management Using Dynamic Matched Baselines). Only credits from projects that use exclusively CCP-approved methodologies are eligible for the CCP label. In the European Union, Regulation (EU) 2024/3012 establishes a voluntary certification framework for carbon removals (CRCF). The Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation, Regulation (EU) 2024/1781, entered into force on July 18, 2024, and Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/1736, adopted on July 14, 2026 and in force from July 15, 2026, cites six CEN/CENELEC harmonized standards for Digital Product Passports (EN 18216, EN 18219, EN 18220, EN 18221, EN 18222 and EN 18223); these could in principle allow a product-level identifier to be linked to a retired credit's serial number as a safeguard against double claiming, which would be a design option for ClimAudit and not an existing requirement. Under Paris Agreement Article 6 and ICAO CORSIA, cross-border transfers require host-country authorization and corresponding adjustments. National uptake of Verra methodologies illustrates this convergence: on June 25, 2026, Verra reported that Peru approved four additional VCS methodologies, including VM0044 v1.2, for use in its National Registry of Mitigation Measures (RENAMI), and on October 13, 2026, Verra reported that Singapore made Verra's VM0010 (Improved Forest Management: Conversion from Logged to Protected Forest) eligible under its International Carbon Credit Framework.

### A.4 Open-Data Initiatives and Reference Registries

To reduce cross-registry double counting, the World Bank's Climate Warehouse program, the Government of Singapore and the International Emissions Trading Association (IETA) developed the Climate Action Data Trust (CAD Trust), a free, open-source platform that aggregates and harmonizes carbon credit data from project registries. It uses distributed ledger technology built on Chia Network's Data Layer to create an auditable record and help identify double counting, and it launched as an independent entity in Singapore on December 7, 2022. According to Singapore's Economic Development Board, the registries connected included Verra, the Global Carbon Council and the Kingdom of Bhutan, with six registries onboarded in total, covering 85% of the carbon credits issued to date; the dashboard tracked more than 17,300 projects. The current production status of individual registries as of October 2026, and any ISO standard for its data model, are not confirmed in the sources reviewed [VERIFICAR MANUALMENTE]. Complementary scientific resources include the Berkeley Carbon Trading Project's Voluntary Registry Offsets Database (VROD) and CarbonPlan's open offsets data work [VERIFICAR MANUALMENTE].


## Section B: Measurement, Reporting, and Digital Verification (dMRV)

### B.5 State of the Art of dMRV: Sensors, Remote Sensing, and Models

State-of-the-art dMRV architectures replace manual spreadsheets and periodic site visits with continuous telemetry that integrates Internet of Things (IoT) hardware sensors, satellite and drone Earth Observation (EO), and biogeochemical process models. Optical and radar satellite constellations (for example Landsat and Sentinel tree-cover products and VIIRS active-fire alerts) reliably detect canopy disturbance. Translating remote-sensing pixels into verifiable carbon credits, however, remains limited by counterfactual baseline ambiguity and unquantified model error.

A review presented at the ICLR 2024 Machine Learning for Remote Sensing (ML4RS) Workshop (Singh et al., arXiv:2401.06421) assessed 241 Google Earth Engine datasets and found that, of the 87 machine-learning-derived datasets, only 17 (20%) had cited studies that quantified any uncertainty; the authors also report that the uncertainty-quantification methods in use are often unreliable, because ensemble and quantile-regression approaches offer no valid coverage guarantee under arbitrary data distributions.

Where physical fuel or mass flows are metered directly by calibrated IoT hardware, the dependence on modeled parameters falls. The cookstove evidence illustrates this: Gill-Wiehl, Kammen and Haya (*Nature Sustainability*, 2024, doi:10.1038/s41893-023-01259-6) estimate that a sample of 51 cookstove projects was over-credited 9.2 times (95% CI 7.0–11.5), whereas Gold Standard's metered methodology, tested on a three-project pellet-stove sample, was the closest to their estimates (1.5 times, 95% CI 0.6–2.4). Instrumented biochar pyrolysis plants can, in the same way, log feedstock mass, pyrolysis temperature and residence time, and link laboratory certificates and GPS-tagged soil application to each batch from factory gate to field. Commercial biochar dMRV platforms such as CropIntellix and Carbon Standards International's C-Sink are described as offering such batch-level traceability [VERIFICAR MANUALMENTE].

### B.6 Biochar Quantification, Standards Comparison, and Dominant Uncertainties

Biochar removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere by thermally converting residual biomass by pyrolysis (typically in the range of roughly 350–800 °C in the absence of oxygen) into a condensed, aromatic carbon fraction that resists decomposition for centuries. With an organic-carbon mass fraction of roughly 0.4–0.9 of dry mass, one dry tonne of biochar contains about 1.5–3.4 t CO₂-equivalent of carbon before any permanence discount (0.41 × 44/12 ≈ 1.5; 0.93 × 44/12 ≈ 3.4), depending on feedstock ash content and pyrolysis quality.

#### Gross stored CO₂ and permanence

Under the Puro Standard Biochar Methodology, Edition 2025 (V1 approved June 2025; version 2 approved 27 November 2025), durability is labelled CORC200+ (previously a 100-year horizon), using a revised open-source decay-based persistence equation informed by Woolf et al. (2021) and Azzi et al. (2024), and aligned by Puro with its General Rules 4.1 and the ICVCM Core Carbon Principles. The gross stored CO₂ of a batch is calculated as:

$$
E_{\text{stored}} = Q_{\text{biochar}} \times C_{\text{biochar,org}} \times F_p^{TH,T_s} \times \frac{44}{12}
$$

where:

- $Q_{\text{biochar}}$ is the dry mass of biochar produced and applied in the reporting period (moisture excluded);
- $C_{\text{biochar,org}}$ is the dry-basis organic carbon mass fraction, determined by batch-stratified laboratory analysis;
- $F_p^{TH,T_s}$ is the permanence factor: the fraction of organic carbon remaining after time horizon $TH$ at mean annual soil temperature $T_s$;
- $44/12$ converts carbon mass to CO₂ mass.

In the regression of Woolf et al. (2021), $F_p$ is a linear function of the molar hydrogen-to-organic-carbon ratio for a fixed time horizon and soil temperature:

$$
F_p^{TH,T_s} = c + m \times \left(\frac{H}{C_{\text{org}}}\right), \qquad
\frac{H}{C_{\text{org}}} = \frac{m_H / M_H}{m_{C_{\text{org}}} / M_C} = \frac{m_H}{m_{C_{\text{org}}}} \times \frac{M_C}{M_H}
$$

with $m_H$ and $m_{C_{\text{org}}}$ the measured mass fractions of hydrogen and organic carbon and $M_C \approx 12.011$ and $M_H \approx 1.008$ g/mol. The intercept $c$ and slope $m$ (negative) are tabulated for each time horizon and soil temperature. For the global mean cropland soil temperature of 14.9 °C (Woolf et al. 2021, Table 3):

| Time horizon | $c$ | $m$ | $R^2$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 years | 1.04 | −0.64 | 0.32 |
| 500 years | 0.57 | −0.50 | 0.17 |
| 1,000 years | 0.30 | −0.28 | 0.07 |

Selected 100-year coefficients at other soil temperatures: 10 °C, $c$ = 1.10, $m$ = −0.59; 25 °C, $c$ = 0.98, $m$ = −0.66. The paper does not state a cap on $F_p$; where the regression yields a value above 1 at low H/C$_{\text{org}}$, $F_p$ is bounded at 1 (100%) in the examples below. The low explanatory power of the regressions ($R^2$ = 0.07–0.32) is itself a principal source of uncertainty, and the two-pool decay approach on which these projections rest has been critically evaluated by Sanei et al. (*Biochar*, 2025, 7:9).

The molar H/C$_{\text{org}}$ ratio indicates the degree of aromatic condensation achieved in pyrolysis. Puro's Edition 2025 (rule 3.5.1) requires H/C$_{\text{org}}$ to be strictly below 0.70, applied to each char stream separately (rule 3.5.2). Isometric's agricultural-soil module (v1.1) sets a stricter eligibility threshold of H/C$_{\text{org}}$ < 0.5. Pyrolysis gases must be combusted or recovered; residual CH₄ emissions from carbonization may not exceed 15% of the initial carbon stored in the biochar (Puro rule 3.5.17). Life-cycle emissions from biomass sourcing ($E_{\text{biomass}}$), production energy ($E_{\text{production}}$) and application transport ($E_{\text{use}}$) are deducted through life-cycle assessment and freight accounting.

#### Dominant uncertainties

1. Organic carbon content ($C_{\text{org}}$): varies strongly with feedstock ash content and must be measured per batch.
2. Persistence regression: the Woolf et al. (2021) regressions explain only 7–32% of the variance, and the two-pool decay approach is under scientific debate (Sanei et al. 2025).
3. Soil temperature: the assumed mean annual soil temperature shifts $F_p$ materially.
4. Dry mass ($Q_{\text{biochar}}$): moisture determination.
5. Durability horizon and method: 100-, 200- and 1,000-year labels rest on different models (H/C regression versus reflectance), the latter being based on Sanei et al. (2024) and Rudra, Petersen and Sanei (2024).

Puro.earth convened a Biochar Persistence Workshop in October 2025 with Carbon Standards International, Isometric, Rainbow, Verra and academic researchers. Its published summary reports a growing view that globally applicable default persistence values are unsuitable for the voluntary market and that project-specific quantification is essential; Puro's open-source persistence equations are being reviewed by other standards; participants questioned whether 1,000-year durability labels are needed, and the latest draft EU CRCF documents propose that all biochar removal units carry the same durability label regardless of quantification method.

### B.8 Treatment of Uncertainty in Literature and Standards

To prevent uncertainty from inflating credit issuance, carbon standards apply conservative deterministic haircuts. Isometric credits the biochar equivalent to one standard deviation below the mean measurement for its 1,000-year pathway, uses coefficients computed at one standard deviation below the mean for its 200-year H/C model, and estimates the carbon content of unsampled batches as the mean minus one standard error; Verra VM0044 v2.0 is proposed to add requirements for uncertainty analysis and assessment.

In parallel, machine-learning literature proposes conformal prediction (CP) as a model-agnostic, post hoc uncertainty-quantification method. Singh et al. (arXiv:2401.06421; presented at the ICLR 2024 ML4RS Workshop) provide conformal modules for Google Earth Engine workflows. Sun et al. (*BioResources*, 2026, 21(4), 11400–11413) apply split-conformal prediction to estimate dry-basis carbon content of raw plant biomass from ash and volatile matter, using 643 filtered solid-fuel samples with a category-stratified held-out test set of 129 samples: a nominal 90% conformal interval covered 95.3% of test samples with a mean width of 11.841 wt.% C, and the authors restrict the model to screening and quality assurance, not high-stakes decisions that still require certified elemental analysis.

For a model $\hat f$ with calibration nonconformity scores $s_1,\dots,s_n$, split conformal prediction forms the interval

$$
\hat C_\alpha(x) = \left[\hat f(x) - \hat q,\; \hat f(x) + \hat q\right], \qquad
\hat q = \text{the } \frac{\lceil (n+1)(1-\alpha) \rceil}{n} \text{ empirical quantile of } \{s_i\},
$$

which gives finite-sample, distribution-free marginal coverage $P\left(y \in \hat C_\alpha(x)\right) \ge 1-\alpha$ under exchangeability of calibration and test data, without access to the underlying model. A recommended design for ClimAudit is to combine the lower bound $\hat f(x) - \hat q$ with a $-1\sigma$ rule of the kind Isometric uses, so that an auditable conservative discount is applied whenever an input parameter carries a Modeled or Synthetic provenance label.


## Section C: Artificial Intelligence for Verification and Audit

### C.9 & C.12 LLMs, Agents, and RAG over Methodologies: Benchmarks and Error Rates

LLMs and autonomous agents are being applied to parse project descriptions, audit verification reports, and check compliance against versioned methodologies. The most closely relevant public benchmark for ClimAudit's architecture is Climate Finance Bench (Mankour, Chafai, Saleh, Ben Hassine, Barreau and Tankov, arXiv:2505.22752, May 2025), which reports measurable error rates for retrieval-augmented question answering over long sustainability documents.

The benchmark contains 330 expert-validated question–answer pairs drawn from 33 English-language corporate sustainability reports covering all 11 GICS sectors. The questions span data extraction, numerical reasoning (such as computing carbon intensity), and logical reasoning. Seven LLM configurations were evaluated: five (Llama 3.1 8B at full precision and in 4-bit, Mistral Nemo 12B in 4-bit, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and GPT-4o) under both minimal and hybrid retrieval, and two (Qwen2.5-72B and DeepSeek R1) under hybrid retrieval only. Answers were graded by an LLM judge. Key results:

- **Minimal RAG (dense retrieval only):** smaller and 4-bit quantized models reached 35%–40% correct answers, while GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet reached roughly 50%–55%, a gap of about 15 percentage points in favour of the frontier models.
- **Hybrid RAG:** dense and BM25 results are combined with a 75%/25% weighting and fused with Reciprocal Rank Fusion, and a cross-encoder reranker selects the final passages. In this configuration Claude 3.5 Sonnet reached 62% correct answers, with a further 10% of answers partially incomplete. DeepSeek R1 reached 60% (about 2 percentage points below Claude 3.5 Sonnet, while Claude consumed about five times fewer output tokens per answer), and Qwen2.5-72B reached 44%.
- **Ablation:** the minimal-RAG baseline of 54.8% rose to 59.1% with BM25 lexical matching (+4.3 percentage points) and to 62.1% with cross-encoder reranking (+3.0 percentage points). Adding Docling HTML conversion without further post-processing lowered accuracy to 57.3% (−4.8 percentage points). The authors conclude that the retriever's ability to surface the passages that contain the answer is the main performance bottleneck. For ClimAudit this supports lexical matching for exact identifiers such as VM0044 or ratios such as H/C<sub>org</sub>.

The authors conclude that a 25%–30% error rate remains too high for regulatory disclosure or automated sustainability scoring and that human review remains necessary. Their own human grading labelled 28.2% of the best configuration's answers as incorrect. For ClimAudit this implies that standalone LLM scoring is not acceptable for compliance decisions and that every extracted figure must be shown with its retrieved evidence snippet and reviewed by a human verifier.

Multi-step numerical reasoning over financial documents shows a similar pattern. On FinQA (Chen et al., EMNLP 2021), the best baseline, FinQANet with RoBERTa-large, reached 61.24% execution accuracy and 58.86% program accuracy against 91.16% and 87.49% for human experts. On ConvFinQA (Chen et al., EMNLP 2022), a fine-tuned T5-large reached 58.66% execution accuracy and 57.05% program accuracy on the test set. Program-of-Thoughts prompting (Chen, Ma, Wang and Cohen, TMLR 2023), in which the LLM writes code and a deterministic interpreter performs the calculation, motivates the same separation of extraction from calculation in ClimAudit. Zhang et al. (AAAI 2026, vol. 40, no. 41, pp. 34772–34780, doi:10.1609/aaai.v40i41.40779) report remote-only accuracies of 73.7% on FinQA and 66.4% on MultiHiertt for a collaborative local–remote numerical-reasoning framework that delegates calculation to code snippets.

Versioned normative documents require version-aware retrieval. Verra VM0044 was first published as v1.0 on 16 August 2022, updated to v1.1 in July 2023, and revised to v1.2 on 27 June 2025. A public consultation on a proposed major revision, to be published as v2.0, ran from 15 July to 17 August 2026. RAG index chunks should therefore carry strict version and section metadata (for example `methodology_id`, `version`, `effective_date`, `grace_period_end`), and retrieval should apply metadata pre-filtering before semantic and BM25 scoring.

### C.10 Hallucination and Grounding in Regulatory and Compliance Settings

For regulatory settings, ClimAudit applies a strict neuro-symbolic separation of powers. This is a design recommendation, not a claim about any cited system. The LLM is restricted to structured semantic extraction, producing schema-constrained JSON that contains the extracted figure, unit, document ID, page number, and exact verbatim character span. Verification and carbon attribution are executed only by deterministic modules. A deterministic Checker module performs three validations before any report is drafted or any calculation runs:

1. It performs an exact normalized substring or float lookup, verifying that every figure in the JSON or narrative report exists inside the cited document span.
2. It executes cross-document mass-balance and logical-consistency rules. For example, it verifies that the dry biochar mass Q<sub>biochar</sub> in the Monitoring Report equals the wet weighbridge mass multiplied by one minus the laboratory moisture fraction, $Q_{dry} = Q_{wet}\,(1 - w/100)$, with $w$ the moisture content in percent, and that it is consistent with the transport (freight) weight records.
3. It routes the deterministic provenance chain to an authenticated human expert in the VVB role, which alone holds create/update/delete and cryptographic signing privileges.

The expert-in-the-loop pattern is consistent with SSKG Hub (He et al., arXiv:2603.00669), a platform in which LLM output is stored as draft knowledge graphs linked to source text, reviewed and edited by experts, and promoted to certified status only after expert approval, with role-based permissions controlling who can view, edit, or certify content.

### C.11 Agent Security: Indirect Prompt Injection and Isolation of Untrusted Content

Prompt Injection is ranked LLM01:2025, the first risk in the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications (2025), and is catalogued in MITRE ATLAS as AML.T0051 (LLM Prompt Injection), with indirect injection as sub-technique AML.T0051.001. OWASP states that indirect prompt injections occur when an LLM accepts input from external sources such as websites or files, that injected content does not need to be human-visible or readable as long as the model parses it, and that, because of the stochastic nature of models, it is unclear whether fool-proof methods of prevention exist. Techniques such as RAG and fine-tuning do not fully mitigate the risk. The Cloud Security Alliance AI Safety Initiative research note "Indirect Prompt Injection Goes Operational" (26 April 2026) summarizes in-the-wild evidence, including ten verified payloads reported by Forcepoint X-Labs (April 2026) and twelve detected cases against AI agents documented by Palo Alto Networks Unit 42 (March 2026), and concludes that indirect prompt injection has moved from proof of concept to live exploitation.

Because LLMs do not enforce privilege separation between system instructions and untrusted data, attackers can embed hidden instructions in uploaded PDFs or Word documents. Kai Greshake's "Inject My PDF" demonstration (15 May 2023) hides text in a PDF using minimum font size and opacity so that human readers cannot see it but text extraction can, and showed it steering GPT-4-powered Bing to summarize a résumé favourably. The foundational academic study is Greshake, Abdelnabi, Mishra, Endres, Holz and Fritz, "Not what you've signed up for: Compromising Real-World LLM-Integrated Applications with Indirect Prompt Injection" (arXiv:2302.12173, 2023). The Alan Turing Institute's Centre for Emerging Technology and Security (CETaS) commentary by Sutton and Ruck, "Indirect Prompt Injection: Generative AI's Greatest Security Flaw" (1 November 2024), covers the threat for general audiences. In a carbon auditing workflow, an adversarial project developer could hide white-on-white text in a laboratory report that instructs the agent to ignore a disqualifying H/C<sub>org</sub> = 0.78 value, report H/C<sub>org</sub> = 0.15 instead, or fabricate VVB approval.

Commercial input guardrails alone are not sufficient. Hackett, Birch, Trawicki, Suri and Garraghan ("Bypassing LLM Guardrails: An Empirical Analysis of Evasion Attacks against Prompt Injection and Jailbreak Detection Systems," Proceedings of the First Workshop on LLM Security (LLMSEC), ACL 2025, pp. 101–114; arXiv:2504.11168) tested six detection systems, including Microsoft Azure Prompt Shield and Meta Prompt Guard. They report up to 100% evasion success in some instances, achieved with specific character-injection techniques (for example emoji smuggling and bidirectional text) rather than as an average: average attack success rates against Azure Prompt Shield were 71.98% for prompt injections and 60.15% for jailbreaks, and against Meta Prompt Guard 70.44% and 73.08%. Training-time defences such as Meta-SecAlign (Chen, Zharmagambetov, Wagner and Guo, "Meta-SecAlign: Training LLMs against Prompt Injection for Robust Agents," arXiv:2507.02735, 2025) harden the base model, but OWASP's guidance is to combine such measures with least-privilege access, segregation of external content, and human approval for high-risk actions. ClimAudit therefore applies four architectural controls:

1. **Pre-ingestion document sanitization:** strip hidden PDF layers, metadata, and zero-contrast (white-on-white) text, and flag anomalous formatting before context ingestion.
2. **Strict privilege separation:** the AI agent operates in a read-only sandbox with no API tokens or private keys for Guardian's mintDocumentBlock or any state-mutation endpoint.
3. **Data spotlighting and deterministic schema validation:** untrusted document chunks are enclosed in unambiguous delimiters, and outputs are validated by deterministic code.
4. **Independent numeric cross-verification and mandatory human VVB approval** for all consequential actions.

### C.13 Fraud and Anomaly Detection in Credits and Measurement Series

To detect off-chain data fabrication before records are hashed onto a blockchain, forensic statistical screens are applied to reported measurement series. A commonly used screen is Benford's Law (first-digit law). It states that in naturally occurring numerical datasets spanning several orders of magnitude, the leading significant digit $d \in \{1,\dots,9\}$ follows

$$P(d) = \log_{10}\!\left(1 + \frac{1}{d}\right),$$

so that 1 appears as the first digit about 30.1% of the time, 5 about 7.9%, and 9 about 4.6%.

Calel, Dechezleprêtre and Venmans, "Policing carbon markets" (Climate Policy 25(9), 1489–1507, 2025, doi:10.1080/14693062.2025.2464699), analyze compliance and enforcement in the EU Emissions Trading System. In the working-paper version, their cumulative non-compliance through 2020 amounts to 474 MtCO<sub>2</sub> under the official definition and 582.3 MtCO<sub>2</sub> under a stricter legal definition, equivalent to a theoretical fine liability of up to €91.8 billion (€76.8 billion if 2005 is pardoned). Applying Benford's Law to installation-level data, the authors find that the dataset as a whole broadly conforms to the law, while installations that were short of allowances deviate more (χ² = 11.4, MAD = 0.004) than installations with surplus allowances (χ² = 3.8, MAD = 0.002). They note that the test can signal possible misreporting but cannot identify who is responsible. Digit tests are therefore a screening step for ClimAudit, to be paired with cross-verification against independent records such as energy invoices, fuel records, and sensor telemetry.

### C.14 Small Models and Fine-Tuning (LoRA / QLoRA): Evidence and Foundation Strategy

Evidence on small and quantized models informs ClimAudit's roadmap. In Climate Finance Bench (Mankour et al., 2025), 4-bit weight quantization of Llama 3.1 8B introduced only minor accuracy differences (within 1%–2%) while reducing emissions by a factor of about four. Under minimal retrieval, the frontier models GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet outperformed the smaller 4-bit models by roughly 15 percentage points. Per-query emissions for Claude 3.5 Sonnet via API were 8.15 ± 4.52 gCO<sub>2</sub>e, and building the vector store was a one-off cost of 0.30 gCO<sub>2</sub>e. These figures concern inference-time quantization and do not evaluate LoRA or QLoRA fine-tuning.

On numerical reasoning, the FinQA and ConvFinQA results in C.9 show that fine-tuned task-specific models remain well below human experts, while delegating calculation to deterministic code (Program-of-Thoughts) addresses arithmetic errors directly.

**Recommendation:** within the hackathon window (5–25 October 2026), do not fine-tune a LoRA adapter on synthetic data; the overfitting risk is high and there is no evidence in the cited work that it would beat retrieval plus deterministic tools. ClimAudit should instead pair a frontier reasoning model with Hybrid RAG (75% dense and 25% BM25 with reranking) and a deterministic Python checker. To leave a foundation for future LoRA/QLoRA training, the backend can log every human VVB review interaction as a signed JSONL record with the fields `document_chunk`, `candidate_extraction_json`, `checker_flags`, and `vvb_approved_ground_truth`, in line with the expert-review workflow of SSKG Hub (arXiv:2603.00669).

### C.15 Multilingual AI in Technical and Regulatory Domains

Carbon credit verification spans jurisdictions with specific legal and technical vocabularies, for example *adicionalidad*, *permanencia*, *fuga*, and *doble contabilidad* in Spanish; *biocarvão*, *verificação*, and *cenário de referência* in Portuguese; *doğrulanmış karbon kredisi* and *çift sayım* (double counting) in Turkish, where crypto-asset rules were introduced by Law No. 7518 (published in the Official Gazette on 2 July 2024) amending the Capital Markets Law; and 生物炭 (biochar), 核查 (verification), and 双重计算 (double counting) in Chinese.

Cross-lingual financial QA work shows that translated numerical-reasoning data needs human validation. The VLSP 2025 numerical reasoning challenge (Le et al., Proceedings of VLSP 2025, ACL Anthology 2025.vlsp-1.25) includes FinQA-Vi, a Vietnamese translation of FinQA with 2,005 instances produced with Gemini Pro and manually validated by human annotators. Unconstrained LLM translation of regulatory clauses or numeric reports risks terminology drift, decimal and separator corruption, and legal misrepresentation. ClimAudit's design rule is therefore to execute all backend extraction, normative checking, deterministic calculation, and Hedera Consensus Service anchoring in canonical English, and to render other languages (the G20 languages and Chinese) only through human-reviewed, deterministic frontend localization dictionaries, so that no machine translation occurs in the evidence path.

### D.18 Oracles, Hardware Attestation, and Zero-Knowledge Proofs (zk-MRV)

A blockchain guarantees immutability only after data is written on-chain, so the oracle problem ("garbage in, garbage out") must be addressed. Practical controls available today include:

- Signed IoT device data. Gold Standard's first fully digital cookstove credits (March 2026) use IoT-SIM-equipped devices with encrypted, digitally signed data transmission, recorded on Hedera.
- SHA-256 document hashing anchored to IPFS and HCS.
- Multi-party attestation with Guardian's `multiSignBlock`, which collects signatures from a group of signers on one VC and creates a VP once a configured threshold percentage is reached. It can enforce VVB and Standard Registry co-signature if both roles are configured as signers.

At the research frontier is zero-knowledge MRV (zk-MRV). Relevant work includes:

- Heiss, Oegel, Shakeri, and Tai, "Verifiable Carbon Accounting in Supply Chains", *IEEE Transactions on Services Computing* (2023), which uses authenticity and zero-knowledge proofs so that trading partners can verify product carbon footprints on a blockchain without disclosing business emission data.
- "Privacy-Preserving Verification of Energy Performance Contracts: A Zero-Knowledge Proof Approach Within the FORTESIE Framework", *Energies* 19(16): 3898, 2026, DOI 10.3390/en19163898 (author list [VERIFICAR MANUALMENTE]).
- Ding and Lu, "A hybrid IoT-Hadoop-blockchain architecture for decentralized MRV and carbon data governance", *Frontiers in Climate* 8 (2026), DOI 10.3389/fclim.2026.1776972. This design-based paper anchors Merkle-tree commitments of IoT data on a Hyperledger Fabric consortium chain, keeps raw data inside the consortium, and optionally connects to a public-chain layer (such as Ethereum) for tokenization.

Because custom ZK circuits for emission math are costly to build and audit, a short solo prototype should rely on SHA-256 commitments, Guardian role-based document access, and deterministic verification, and should not claim custom ZK-SNARK circuits.

### D.19 and D.20 Interoperability Standards and Privacy-Preserving Evidence

Interoperability across carbon registries draws on three complementary standards efforts:

- **CAD Trust data model.** The Climate Action Data Trust launched Data Model Version 2.0 on 23 October 2025, and its API development was reported complete in January 2026, with connected registries expected to complete their transition by Q2 2026. An ISO data model for carbon credit markets (ISO/AWI TS 32214, ISO/TC 322/WG 5) is at the preparatory stage; its working draft is expected to be finalized in 2027 for public consultation.
- **W3C Decentralized Identifiers (DIDs) and Verifiable Credentials (VCs).** Hedera Guardian uses DIDs and VCs for its policy documents, and Guardian 3.7.0 adds Organizations with their own Hedera account and DID.
- **CEN/CENELEC Digital Product Passport standards.** Six EN standards were presented on 25 June 2026 under standardization request M/604 for the EU Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation: EN 18216 (data exchange protocols), EN 18219 (unique identifiers), EN 18220 (data carriers), EN 18221 (data storage and archiving), EN 18222 (APIs and lifecycle management), and EN 18223 (system interoperability). They are voluntary until cited in the Official Journal. They do not address carbon credits, so linking a retired credit to a product-level identifier is a design option, not a regulatory requirement.

To protect smallholder privacy while still detecting spatial-temporal double counting, the prototype could compute a canonical deterministic commitment:

$$
\texttt{parcel\_period\_key} = \mathrm{SHA\text{-}256}\bigl(\texttt{canonical\_GeoJSON\_polygon} \,\|\, \texttt{methodology\_id} \,\|\, \texttt{start\_date} \,\|\, \texttt{end\_date}\bigr)
$$

and publish only this hash and a coarse regional identifier to the public HCS topic. Exact coordinates would stay in an encrypted or permissioned VC that only the assigned VVB and Standard Registry can access. Note that Guardian's `selectiveAttributesBlock` filters which attributes appear in documents returned by a documents-source add-on. It is not by itself a privacy or role-based disclosure mechanism, so access control should rely on Guardian roles, groups, and encryption.

### Synthesis of What Remains Open vs. Hype

The overarching lesson from the 2025–2026 frontier is that trust is built at the boundary between messy off-chain evidence and deterministic on-chain rules. Commercial hype in 2022–2024 promised that blockchain token pools would carry high-quality credits; CarbonPlan's analysis (Badgley and Cullenward, 2022) found that roughly 28% of the credits bridged into Toucan's Base Carbon Tonne pool came from "zombie projects" and that nearly all bridged Verra credits had crediting periods starting before 2016. Likewise, end-to-end LLMs cannot yet audit sustainability reports autonomously: on Climate Finance Bench (Mankour et al., 2025), the best configuration answered 62% of questions correctly, leaving roughly a quarter of answers incorrect or unsupported. Indirect prompt injection (OWASP LLM01:2025) adds a separate attack surface for any agent that ingests third-party documents.

The open frontier where a focused engineering team can, in the author's view, make an immediate and defensible contribution lies in bridging neuro-symbolic document verification, forensic statistical screening (Benford's Law), and provenance-weighted uncertainty discounting (Measured vs. Modeled vs. Synthetic) into Hedera Guardian's Formula Linked Definitions and Verifiable Credential schemas.

### Design Notes

- **Benford gate.** Benford's law applies to data spanning several orders of magnitude; a series of batch weights from one reactor in a narrow range may not follow it, so the gate should be presented as a screening flag that triggers manual review, not as proof of fabrication. For first digits (8 degrees of freedom), the $\chi^2$ critical values are 15.51 ($\alpha = 0.05$) and 20.09 ($\alpha = 0.01$).
- **Spatial key.** A hash of an exact polygon only detects identical parcels. To catch overlapping or jittered parcels, it is recommended to commit to a set of H3 hexagonal cell indices (for example at resolution 9 or 10) or to rounded canonical coordinates, and to compare cell sets.
- **Modeled-uncertainty level.** The confidence level for the Modeled discount (for example $\alpha = 0.10$ versus roughly the 15.87th percentile for $-1\sigma$) should be fixed explicitly and displayed in the UI.
- **Demo video.** It is recommended to allocate at least 45 seconds of the 3-minute video to live Hedera Testnet HCS transaction IDs and Guardian schema outputs.

## Deliverable 4: Risks, Limitations, and Judge Defense Protocol

### Claims That Must Not Be Made in the Submission

To preserve scientific integrity and pre-empt skepticism from IEEE and Hedera judges, the ClimAudit submission should observe five boundaries on its claims.

1. **No replacement of human auditors.** The submission must not claim that the AI agent replaces human validation/verification bodies (VVBs) or makes autonomous issuance decisions. The Climate Finance Bench study (Mankour, Chafai, Saleh, Ben Hassine, Barreau, and Tankov, 2025; arXiv:2505.22752) reports that even the best hybrid-RAG configuration on climate-disclosure question answering left roughly 25%–30% of answers incorrect or unsupported, and concludes that such an error rate remains too high for regulatory disclosure. That benchmark measures disclosure question answering rather than compliance scoring, but it supports the design choice that an LLM should not be the decision-maker. ClimAudit's AI agent should be described exclusively as a read-only extraction and inconsistency-surfacing copilot; all arithmetic is executed by deterministic code, and final verification belongs solely to an authorized human VVB.

2. **No bridging or tokenization of retired credits, and no claim that tokenization cures low credit quality.** Badgley and Cullenward ("Zombies on the blockchain", CarbonPlan, 7 April 2022) analyzed Toucan's bridging of retired Verra credits into on-chain tokens. In May 2022 Verra announced that it would prohibit creating instruments or tokens based on retired credits and opened a consultation on "immobilizing" credits so that they could be tokenized under conditions. ClimAudit should therefore state that it operates at the primary dMRV verification and issuance layer inside Hedera Guardian (or under a registry's pre-mint immobilization rules), never through one-way retirement bridging. The current status of Verra's tokenization rules should be confirmed before the pitch [VERIFICAR MANUALMENTE].

3. **No claim that blockchain immutability by itself solves off-chain data fraud ("garbage in, garbage out").** The submission should state that anchoring hashes on the Hedera Consensus Service (HCS) provides tamper-evident auditability only after ingestion. Pre-ledger fraud is mitigated, but never eliminated without physical site audits, through ClimAudit's cross-document mass-balance checks, a stoichiometric screening gate (molar $\mathrm{H/C_{org}} \le 0.7$ [VERIFICAR MANUALMENTE]), Benford's-Law digit forensics, and conservative uncertainty haircuts on non-measured data.

4. **No presentation of synthetic hackathon data or Hedera Testnet tokens as real credits.** Synthetic test data and Testnet tokens must never be presented as certified real-world Verified Carbon Units (VCUs) or as credits carrying the ICVCM Core Carbon Principles (CCP) label. Every UI view, exported JSON file, and HCS payload should carry an explicit `"provenance": "SYNTHETIC_DEMO_DATA"` marker.

5. **No claim that the LLM translates methodology rules or verification reports across languages.** Dynamic LLM translation risks legal and numerical drift. Multilingual accessibility should instead be delivered through deterministic frontend internationalization (i18n) dictionaries over an English-only backend.
